# Deliverable 2: User interviews

Interview IDs are INT-01 to INT-04 and paragraph IDs are P01, P02 and so on, so a claim is cited as INT-02:P04. No names or identifying details are kept; people are referred to by interview ID and role.

All four interviews were conducted virtually by the respective team members with participants relevant to our target role. The team collectively reviewed the interview notes prior to incorporating any observations into the evidence log. Documentation of the interview process and related records is maintained in LEDGER.md Entry 1.

## 2a. Interview questions

We started from the five questions in our proposal (Appendix C) and revised them. We added an opening question about the person’s situation, three new past-event questions about follow-ups, reminder timing and misapplied payments, and a closing question. Questions 2 to 9 each ask about a specific past event.

**Open with their situation**

1.  Tell me about your role. How many communities or homes do you work with, and what does a normal week look like?

**Dig into real events**

2.  Tell me about the last maintenance request a resident sent you. How did it reach you, and what happened between then and the day it was closed?
3.  Think of the last time a resident followed up more than once on the same request. What were they asking, and how did you answer?
4.  Think of the last notice you sent to the residents of more than one community. What did you do, step by step?
5.  When did a board member last ask you for a document? Where was it, and how long did it take to get it to them?
6.  Has a board-only document or message ever reached someone it was not meant for? What happened the last time?
7.  Think of last month’s dues for one of your communities. How did the payments come in, and what did you do about the ones that were late?
8.  Tell me about the last late-payment reminder you sent. When did it go out, and what decided that timing?
9.  Tell me about the last time a payment was recorded against the wrong home or counted twice. How did you notice, and how was it fixed?

**Close**

10. Who else should we talk to about this, and is there anything we should have asked but didn’t?

## 2c. Raw notes

### INT-01: Portfolio community manager, management company, 7 HOAs

- **P01** Manages seven HOAs for a management company, ranging from 90 to 310 homes, about 1,100 homes in total. “Most of my day is email. On a normal week I’d guess 60 to 80 emails a day.”
- **P02** Each community has its own email distribution list, its own shared-drive folder, and its own tab in a requests spreadsheet. “Each community has its own folder, its own list, its own everything.”
- **P03** Last request: on a Tuesday last week, a resident emailed a photo of a broken pool gate latch. She forwarded it to the pool vendor and logged it in her spreadsheet. The vendor fixed it on Thursday. “I forgot to tell her it was fixed. She emailed me again on Monday asking if anyone had looked at it.”
- **P04** “That happens all the time. Probably a third of my email is people asking ‘any update?’ on something I already handled.”
- **P05** Last week she answered a resident of one community while the spreadsheet tab of another community was open, and told the resident a landscaper was scheduled. The landscaper was for the other community. “I had the wrong tab open. I had to send an apology email.”
- **P06** Two weeks ago she announced a holiday office closure. She sent the same email seven times, once to each community’s list. “Seven emails, same text, and I still missed one community. Their board president called me.”
- **P07** Last month a board member asked for the reserve study. It was in the shared drive, but in the previous year’s folder. It took her about 40 minutes to find and send.
- **P08** Earlier this year a draft budget sat in a board folder. A board member forwarded the share link to a neighbor, and the neighbor posted a screenshot in the community’s Facebook group before the board had voted. “The link worked for anyone who had it. That was the problem.”
- **P09** Residents pay by check to a lockbox or through the bank’s website. Each month she downloads the bank report and matches payments to homes in a spreadsheet. “When someone pays two months at once, or pays the wrong amount, I have to figure out where it goes by hand.”
- **P10** She sends late reminders after the grace period in each community’s governing documents. “It’s not the same everywhere. One of mine is 10 days, another is 15, one is 30 because their bylaws say so.” Last month she sent reminders on the 16th for one community.
- **P11** In August a payment was posted to unit 14 instead of unit 41, and the owner of unit 41 received a late notice. “She was furious, and she had a right to be.”
- **P12** Suggested we talk to the accounting coordinator at her company, who posts payments, and to a board treasurer.
- **P13** At the end she asked what we were building. After a one-line answer: “Oh, that would save me so much time. I’d use that every day.” (Moved to the compliments and predictions list.)

### INT-02: On-site community manager, one condominium association, 420 units

- **P01** Manages one 420-unit condominium building from an office in the lobby. “People just walk into my office. That’s half my requests.”
- **P02** Last request: on a Sunday a resident called the after-hours line about water dripping from her bathroom ceiling. The on-call technician came on Monday and found the leak in the unit above. The request was written on paper and typed into the work-order system later. “The tenant called, not the owner. The owner lives in Florida.”
- **P03** About a third of the units are rented. “Tenants report most of the problems because they live there. But I can’t talk to them about the owner’s account.”
- **P04** The building has a work-order system, but residents cannot see it. “They can’t see the status, so they come down and ask me.”
- **P05** Last week she announced a water shutoff with printed flyers on every floor and an email. Some residents said they never saw it. “Every time there’s a shutoff, someone says nobody told them.”
- **P06** Residents ask her for the rules and regulations and the pet policy, and she emails the PDF. Last month she sent the pet policy “at least five times.”
- **P07** Most owners pay by automatic ACH through the bank, and a few by check. Last month 23 units were late.
- **P08** “We don’t send anything until day 15. Our declaration says late fees start on the 15th, so a reminder before that just makes people angry.” Last year an owner complained after an early reminder from the previous management company.
- **P09** In June an owner set up automatic payment with the bank and also mailed a check, so he paid twice. The refund took three weeks. “He wanted his money back the same day.”
- **P10** When an ACH payment is returned, the bank reports it about 3 to 5 business days later. “By then we’ve already counted it as paid.”
- **P11** Suggested we talk to a board treasurer and to the person at the company who posts payments.
- **P12** “Honestly, anything that stops people walking into my office would be great.” (Moved to the compliments and predictions list.)

### INT-03: Volunteer HOA board treasurer, 160-home townhome community

- **P01** Volunteer treasurer for three years. The community uses a management company, and the board meets monthly.
- **P02** Before the last board meeting he asked the manager for the delinquency report two days ahead and received the spreadsheet on the morning of the meeting. “I’m presenting numbers I’ve had for two hours.”
- **P03** That report listed one owner as delinquent who had already paid; the payment had not been posted yet. “We almost sent that account to collections.”
- **P04** “Our draft budgets and anything about lawsuits have to stay with the board until we vote.” Last year a draft special assessment went to the whole community when a board member hit reply-all on the wrong email thread.
- **P05** Board members email documents to each other as attachments. “I have four versions of the same budget in my inbox.”
- **P06** Residents email him directly about their dues balances, which is not his job. Last month three residents did.
- **P07** He thinks a reminder should go out a few days after the due date, before any late fee. “Most people who are late just forgot.”
- **P08** The board decided not to accept credit cards because of processing fees of about 3%, although residents asked for it. “If we take cards, who pays the fee? The association can’t absorb it.”
- **P09** The board president wants to approve announcements before the manager sends them. Last month the manager sent new pool rules the board had not approved.
- **P10** Suggested we talk to residents and to the management company’s accounting team.
- **P11** “If I could see who’s paid without asking, that’d be worth it on its own. Everyone on the board would use it.” (Moved to the compliments and predictions list.)

### INT-04: Accounting and administrative coordinator, management company, 12 communities

- **P01** Posts payments for 12 communities, updates owner accounts when homes are sold, and answers the company’s support inbox.
- **P02** Last month there were nine home sales across her communities. One was missed, so the old owner kept receiving statements and the new owner received none for two months. “Nobody told us about the closing.”
- **P03** “We only find out somebody moved in when the title company sends us the paperwork.”
- **P04** Each morning she downloads the lockbox and bank files and matches payments to accounts. Last Friday six checks had no account number on the memo line, and matching them took about an hour.
- **P05** For two of her communities the board asked for a courtesy reminder 5 days after the due date, before the late fee. “It depends on the board. Some want a courtesy reminder, some don’t want anything until the late fee.”
- **P06** In September a reminder went to an owner whose payment had arrived that morning but was not posted yet. “He called to say he’d paid, and he had.”
- **P07** When an ACH payment is returned, she reverses it and charges the returned-payment fee set by that community. “Every community has its own fee.”
- **P08** The most common support questions are password resets for the bank’s website and “how do I see my balance?”
- **P09** A former board member still had access to the board’s shared folder six months after leaving the board. “Nobody removed him. We found out when he commented on something.”
- **P10** Suggested we talk to residents who would pay by card, and to the company’s IT administrator.

## 2f. What we learned

The most useful thing we learned is that there is no single right time to send a late-payment reminder. One manager waits until day 15 because late fees start then (INT-02:P08), a treasurer wants a courtesy reminder a few days after the due date (INT-03:P07), and the coordinator says it depends on each board (INT-04:P05). Two interviewees also described reminders going to people who had already paid (INT-03:P03, INT-04:P06). This changed our plan: the proposal fixed the reminder at 5 days after the due date, and now each community has its own GRACE_PERIOD_DAYS setting, and the reminder run checks for a recorded payment immediately before it sends anything (S-7).
